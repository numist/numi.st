My grandparents, uncles, aunts, sister, and BIL were all union. Strikes—even their threat—are a powerful tool when negotiating with management. I am pro-union, and I support strikers.

Taking a day off work is not a strike.

Power will only ever recognize threats to power. Strikes threaten a company's bottom line. If everyone takes a day off work, the effect on production can be completely eliminated by the elasticity of work. This is especially true for exempt[^hourly] employees, who are usually expected to deliver regardless of time off and have been indoctrinated over their careers into making up for the lost time. In fact, the evolution of the economy during the era of modern trade unions has made strike effectiveness more complicated—a company can weather a strike for longer if it has distributed production across locations that aren't likely to engage in secondary actions. A strike at a software company will not be as immediately effective as a strike in a service industry, as made evident by no-strike clauses in healthcare, the Railway Labor Act,[^rla], and the effective union-busting of air traffic controllers.[^atc]

But that doesn't mean that a daylong walkout—like what we're seeing in Minneapolis today—won't be effective. It's just not a strike; it's a protest.

While it's easy to be tricked into thinking that nonviolent protests don't work, the reality is that they are a visible shot across the bow of power. Protests are a reversion to direct democracy, a show of numbers by people united by a common grievance when their elected representatives fall short. They bring like-minded people together.

Most importantly, protests are a threat of escalation. The ["3.5% rule" in political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3.5%25_rule) is intended to assess the credibility of that threat, but it's not founded on any specific principles that make it inevitable. If you're unhappy with things, get out there and meet people who share your view. Plan actions together that will threaten power in ways that encourage better outcomes.

[^hourly]: As opposed to hourly/shift
[^rla]: See: [Biden administration's blockade of a railroad strike in 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_United_States_railroad_labor_dispute)
[^atc]: See: [the Reagan administration's strike-breaking actions against air traffic controllers in 1981](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_Professional_Air_Traffic_Controllers_Organization_strike)
